Why appeal instead of just disputing again

An accepted verdict isn't final until the finality window closes. Appealing during this window doesn't restart the docket from scratch — it escalates the same adjudication to a new, larger group of validators, who first vote on whether the original result should even be re-evaluated.

If they agree it should be, a new leader re-assesses the evidence and the combined validator set votes again. This can escalate through multiple rounds, each doubling the validator count, until a decision holds.

Appeal bond

Appeals require a bond, covered by whoever initiates the appeal. If the appeal succeeds — the verdict is overturned — you get the bond back plus a reward. If it fails, the bond is forfeited.
